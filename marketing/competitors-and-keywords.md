# Holo Folder: competitors and keyword research

Researched 14 November 2026. Read-only desk research using web search, App Store listings (UK storefront where possible), Google autocomplete (gl=uk) and Google Trends (12 months, GB and worldwide). No accounts were created. Unless a line says otherwise, facts come from the cited page. Lines marked **Estimate** are my judgement. I had no keyword-volume tool: the Ahrefs connector needs authorising in claude.ai connector settings, so demand is given as tiers built from Trends ratios and autocomplete, not absolute monthly volumes.

## TL;DR

The closest competitor is **Dex (dextcg.com)**, and it overlaps a lot. It has virtual binders, a camera scanner that reads a whole binder page in one capture, apps on iOS, Android and web, multi-source prices converted to your currency, and a Dex+ plan at **£3.99/month and £34.99/year, the same as Holo Folder**. Its free tier allows unlimited cards. Holo Folder's 100-entry free cap is the weakest part of its offer, because nearly every major competitor lets you log an unlimited collection for free.

Several features that might look unique are not. pkmn.gg (Pro) and Dex both have binder views. CollectHolo already offers an MCP connection for ChatGPT and Claude on its PRO tier. Pokécardex works offline. The features I could not find anywhere else are **Show mode**, meaning a deal checker with pay-up-to prices, a hunting list and a purchase tally, and **partner collections that two people edit together**. GBP-native, confidence-gated pricing is partly differentiated: the UK-focused alternatives are price-guide websites, not collection apps.

The best-value search clusters for a small site are **binder layout and planner terms**, **National Dex binder and placeholder terms**, **UK price long-tail terms**, and **card show tips**. Generic "app", "scanner" and "card value" head terms are held by large brands and vendor listicles.

### Acquisition channels

These are a mix of verified points and estimates.

- **Collectr (verified):** pays for YouTube sponsorships. SponsorRadar tracks 10 sponsored videos across 7 channels (PokiChloe, UnlistedLeaf, FaZe Rug), the most recent in July 2026, plus 64 placements mostly with Phillips Collectibles (about 110k subscribers) as an ongoing sponsor ([sponsorradar](https://sponsorradar.com/brands/collectr), [collectr-1](https://sponsorradar.com/brands/collectr-1)). One anecdotal creator rate is $200 per video ([skool](https://www.skool.com/cardboard-empire-8639/pokemv-portfolio-review)). Ranking #10 in Reference also means it gets App Store browse traffic.
- **Dex (verified):** the homepage uses creator testimonials and has indexable `/expansions/...` and `/cards/...` pages. **Estimate:** organic search plus App Store search are its main channels.
- **Card Codex (verified):** affiliate programme paying $5 per referral plus 25% recurring ([cardcodex](https://cardcodex.com/pokemon-card-affiliate-programme/)). It also writes card-show content, such as negotiation guides.
- **CollectHolo, MyDex, Eyevo, PokeScope (verified):** each runs "best Pokémon card apps 2026" listicles and "X vs Y" comparison pages. Most of the "best app" search results are made of these vendor pages.
- **Pokellector, TCG Collector, PriceCharting (estimate):** years of SEO on set and card pages, driving traffic into their apps.
- **Binder planners (verified):** Product Hunt launches (AuraBinder, Binderdex), Elite Fourum threads, Etsy for placeholder printables.
- **Reddit:** search did not return Reddit threads directly. Elite Fourum threads show the common pattern of "Pokellector for binder sets, Collectr for value". **Estimate:** r/PokemonTCG and r/pkmntcgcollections are where these choices get discussed, but I can't quantify mention share.

### Positioning angles most likely to win

1. **"Your real binder, page by page, priced in pounds."** Combine binder-first with UK/GBP. Dex is binder-ish but global. The UK price sites have no binder. Nobody owns the UK collector who keeps a physical binder.
2. **"Card-show mode."** It is unique, easy to demo in a 15-second TikTok or Short ("is this a good deal?"), and fits the content gap that card show tips searches reveal. UK shows and cons (Pokémon events, local TCG fairs) make good small-scale sponsorship or stall opportunities.
3. **"One collection for two."** Partner and family shared collections suit parents and kids or couples. This is an angle nobody else uses and it fits the existing families audience page.

I'd drop "AI/API" and "offline" as headline angles. Both are real features, but competitors have them and they don't drive search demand.

### Best-value clusters for a small site

1. **Binder layout planner + National Dex binder.** Ship a free, no-signup interactive planner page and a free printable National Dex placeholder download. Both compete against hobby tools and Etsy, not big brands, and they lead straight into the product. The autocomplete shows people searching for a _tool_, which is exactly what this product is.
2. **"<Set> binder layout / master set binder" for each new release**, starting now with Delta Reign while its results are still settling, then 30th Celebration and Flowing Fists. Prepare the Flowing Fists page in January ahead of 5 Feb 2027 (date unconfirmed). Present the set as binder pages, which plain checklists don't. Skip plain "<set> checklist pdf" head terms.
3. **UK price long-tail:** "pokemon card value uk", "check pokemon card prices uk", "pokemon card price checker uk", and per-card GBP pages for chase cards in current sets. No big brand owns these, and they suit GBP-native pricing.
4. **Card show tips (UK angle):** "first pokemon card show tips", "how to negotiate at a card show", UK show calendars, each linking to Show mode. Low competition and a unique product match.

Avoid as primary targets: "pokemon card value", "pokemon card scanner", "pokemon card tracker app", "best pokemon card app". These are held by PriceCharting and TCGplayer or flooded with vendor listicles. A single honest "Holo Folder vs Dex / Collectr / Pokellector" comparison page is still worth it, since every competitor has one.

## 4. Pricing and positioning implications

| Product             | Monthly       | Yearly                        | Free tier                       |
| ------------------- | ------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| **Holo Folder**     | £3.99         | £34.99                        | 100 entries, 1 binder           |
| Dex+                | £3.99 / £4.99 | £34.99 / £44.99, lifetime £99 | **Unlimited cards**, 3 folders  |
| Collectr PRO        | £7.99         | £59.99                        | Unlimited core, limited scans   |
| PriceCharting (app) | $5.99         | $59.99                        | Unlimited tracker               |
| Pokellector Plus    | $4.99         | $49.99                        | Checklist free                  |
| TCG Collector       | $3.99         | ~$40                          | Unlimited (1 language per card) |
| Pokécardex          | €3.99         | €39.99                        | Free core                       |
| Shiny Pro           | ~$4.99        | lifetime ~$99.99              | Free core                       |

- **£3.99/£34.99 is competitive.** It matches Dex+ exactly, is about half of Collectr, and sits below PriceCharting and Pokellector. There's no case for lowering it, and since it matches the closest rival there's not much room to raise it either.
- **The 100-entry free cap is the real problem.** One Perfect Order master set is 203 cards. A National Dex binder is 1,025 slots. A visitor arriving from a "national dex binder" or "master set binder" search hits the cap in minutes, while Dex, PriceCharting and TCG Collector let them log everything for free. **Recommendation (estimate):** gate on things competitors charge for or don't have, such as extra binders, Partner mode, Show mode pay-up-to and deal verdicts, sold-price comparisons and API/MCP. Then either remove the entry cap or raise it a lot, for example to one full binder of any size. The 7-day trial helps, but it doesn't fix a comparison that shows up on day one.
- **Lifetime licence:** it is mentioned on the site but not priced. Dex charges £99 and Shiny about $99.99, so a £79-£99 lifetime price would be in line with the market and suits people who dislike subscriptions.
- **No app store presence** costs Holo Folder the store browse traffic that puts Collectr at #10. A thin store wrapper (TWA on Google Play, or similar) is worth considering later. It is out of scope here and flagged only as a gap.
