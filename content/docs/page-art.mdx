---
title: Page art
description: Lay one picture behind several pockets of a binder page (the Michi method), with your cards on top.
---

Page art lays one picture behind a rectangle of pockets, from a single pocket up to the whole page. Empty pockets under
the picture lose their own edge and show their part of it, so neighbouring pockets join into one picture without a
seam. Cards sit on top in their own pockets, and taking a card out shows the picture again. Collectors call this the
Michi method.

In the app, open the binder's **Storage** menu and choose **Edit layout**, select the pockets you want (or none), then **More options → Page art on page N**.
Drag across the pockets on the small page, or tap two corners, then choose a picture:

- **Card**: any card in our catalogue, by name, set or number.
- **Pokémon**: a Pokémon's official artwork.
- **Your image**: a PNG, JPEG or WebP under 5 MB, kept in your private artwork store.

Fill or fit the area, zoom and move the picture, then save. A new picture replaces any other on the page it overlaps.
Partners can add and change page art on the owner's binders.

Each picture is attached to the pocket at its top-left corner, so it moves with that pocket when you move a page, add
pages or undo a move. A picture that runs past the edge of the page is cut to fit. If that pocket is removed, its
picture goes too.

## Shares and offline

Shared links show page art, including your own uploaded images, which are served only while the share is on and the
binder uses them. Page art shows offline once the binder has opened on the device; uploaded images are kept on the
device for that. Adding or changing page art needs a connection.

## Printing

**Print this page's art** prints each pocket's piece at 63 × 88 mm, nine to a sheet on A4 or US Letter, with cut
marks and a 50 mm line to check the scale. Print at 100% ("Actual size"). Set **Gap between pockets** to the distance
from the edge of one sleeved card to the edge of the next in your binder: the print leaves that strip out of the
picture, so once the pieces are in their pockets the picture lines up across the welds. Pockets that already hold a
card are skipped unless you turn that off. Each piece carries a small "PAGE ART" label.

Catalogue pictures are small (about 480 to 600 pixels wide), so they look soft printed across a large area. Your own
image prints sharper.

## REST and MCP

`list_binders` returns each binder's `pageArt`. Set it with `update_binder` (`PATCH /app/api/v1/binders/{id}`), which
replaces the whole list; send `null` or `[]` to clear it:

```json
{
  "id": "00000000-0000-4000-8000-000000000001",
  "pageArt": [
    {
      "id": "top-row",
      "anchorSlotId": "slot-a",
      "rows": 1,
      "cols": 3,
      "source": { "kind": "pokemon", "pokemonId": 25 },
      "frame": { "fit": "fill", "zoom": 1, "x": 0, "y": 0 }
    }
  ]
}
```

- `anchorSlotId` is the slot id of the top-left pocket, from `list_storage_slots`. It must be a pocket of this binder
  (`409` otherwise).
- `rows` and `cols` are 1 to 8. A binder holds at most 200 pictures.
- `source` is `{ "kind": "card", "cardId", "image" }` with the card's image from `get_card`, `{ "kind": "pokemon",
"pokemonId" }`, or `{ "kind": "upload", "id" }` with the `artworkRef` from `prepare_artwork_upload` after uploading the
  bytes (`400` if the upload isn't there).
- `frame` is optional: `fit` is `fill` or `fit`, `zoom` 0.25 to 4, `x` and `y` -1 to 1.

Until the database migration is applied, `update_binder` with `pageArt` returns `503`.
